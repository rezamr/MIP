# MIP First Operational Playbook v0.1

## Status

`EXPLORATORY OPERATIONAL BASELINE — NOT YET CONFIRMATORY`

## Purpose

This file defines exactly what MIP should do from zero for the first standardized REQUEST sessions.

The objective is not to re-prove every historical claim. The objective is to build a repeatable MIP communication/request procedure, compare it against historical and control audio conditions, and preserve enough structure that later improvements can be attributed to specific protocol changes.

Operational words such as `MATRIX`, `request`, `inject`, `send`, and `communication` describe the working task. They do not establish an external substrate or causal mechanism.

Initial alphabet:

The app precommits:

- audio condition;
- REQUEST encoding protocol version;
- RNG provider;
- nonce;

# Standard environment

Before starting:

- quiet room;
- comfortable lying or reclined position;
- stereo headphones correctly oriented left/right;
- phone notifications and calls disabled;
- no alcohol/recreational drugs;
- no deliberate sleep deprivation;
- no aggressive hyperventilation;
- room temperature comfortable;
- no need for complete darkness, but reduce distracting light;
- do not begin if the participant feels unable to maintain ordinary voluntary control and orientation.

The participant must remain able to stop the session voluntarily.

Termination command:

`END SESSION`

# Standard session timeline

The target timeline is approximately 30–35 minutes. Exact UTC event times are logged by the app.

## Stage 0 — Baseline / commitment — 2 minutes

The app creates the commitment and session files.

Before audio begins, record:

- alertness 0–10;
- fatigue 0–10;
- anxiety 0–10;
- concentration 0–10;
- emotional intensity 0–10;
- expected success 0–10.

Do not inspect RNG state or future output.

## Stage 1 — Audio start / passive settling — 2 minutes

Start the assigned stereo audio.

Do not immediately try to communicate.

Simply notice the sound and allow breathing to become slow and comfortable.

No target imagery yet.

## Stage 2 — Energy Conversion Box analogue — 1–2 minutes

Create a simple mental container outside the immediate body space.

Place into it, conceptually rather than visually if necessary:

- ordinary worries;
- the desire to prove the experiment;
- fear of failure;
- excitement about a possible hit;
- thoughts about previous sessions;
- guesses about what the machine will do.

Close the container.

The purpose is attentional separation, not a claim that a literal object exists.

If imagery is weak, use the felt idea: `these concerns are temporarily set aside`.

## Stage 3 — Resonant breathing/tuning analogue — approximately 3 minutes

Use approximately six slow comfortable breath cycles.

Suggested pattern:

- inhale gently through the nose;
- brief comfortable pause only if natural;
- exhale slowly;
- optionally use a soft sustained `ah`, `oh`, or `umm` on exhalation if comfortable.

Do not breathe rapidly or deeply enough to produce dizziness or tingling from hyperventilation.

During inhalation, use the simple felt representation: `energy/air enters and the body becomes calm and alert`.

During exhalation, use: `unnecessary tension leaves`.

Do not force a color.

## Stage 4 — REBAL / circulating-field analogue — 2–3 minutes

Use a simple circulation model without forcing visual imagery.

Operational representation:

1. Sense or imagine a flow beginning near the feet/lower body.
2. Allow it to move upward through the body toward the head/crown.
3. Allow it to move outward around the body.
4. Let it circulate back downward around the outside and return toward the feet/lower body.
5. Maintain a complete surrounding loop/field.

Important controls:

- no required color;
- no required brightness;
- no requirement to see the body from outside;
- no requirement to feel a literal physical energy;
- if a spontaneous color, shape, pulse, rotation, or external viewpoint appears, observe it without deliberately changing it and record it afterward.

The function in v0.1 is attentional/state stabilization and continuity with prior successful subjective sessions.

## Stage 5 — Affirmation / task orientation — 30–60 seconds

Use one short neutral orientation statement internally:

`I remain aware, in control, and open to observing what occurs. I will make one clear request and record the result exactly.`

Do not add claims about external beings, guarantees, protection from supernatural harm, or expected success.

## Stage 6 — Focus-10-style relaxation — 5–7 minutes

Count slowly from `1` to `10`.

With each number, relax progressively while preserving mental alertness.

Suggested progression:

- 1–2: feet and legs;
- 3–4: pelvis and abdomen;
- 5–6: chest, back, shoulders;
- 7–8: arms, hands, neck, jaw;
- 9: face and eyes;
- 10: whole body deeply relaxed while attention remains awake.

Do not attempt to become unconscious.

At `10`, use the internal label:

`Body deeply relaxed; mind awake.`

Remain responsive enough to terminate the session if needed.

## Stage 7 — State stabilization — 2 minutes

Do not think about the requested bit yet.

Notice only:

- body stillness;
- alertness;
- auditory phenomena;
- vibration/pulse;
- rotation;
- spontaneous imagery;
- perceived presence/agency;
- time distortion.

Do not chase any phenomenon.

If something spontaneous appears, allow it to pass unless the protocol explicitly requires interaction.

The app then displays the randomly assigned requested bit.

## Stage 8 — Semantic lock — approximately 20–30 seconds

Use exactly:

`At the predefined target time, the system output is X.`

Replace `X` with the assigned `0` or `1`.

Repeat the exact sentence internally three times, slowly.

Do not add explanations, bargaining, alternate outcomes, or questions.

## Stage 9 — Symbolic encoding — 20–30 seconds

Represent only the requested bit.

For `1`:

- simple upright numeral `1`.

For `0`:

- simple circular numeral `0`.

Preferred representation order:

1. visual numeral if it appears naturally;
2. otherwise internal verbal label `one` / `zero`;
3. otherwise a stable kinesthetic/spatial sense of the symbol;
4. otherwise abstract certainty of the requested value.

Do not add colors, spaceships, people, scenes, future-life imagery, or complex stories.

Do not force an image if imagery is difficult.

## Stage 10 — Controlled affective tag — 10–20 seconds

Attach a controlled feeling of completion/certainty to the already defined bit.

Target feeling:

`This is the intended completed outcome.`

Intensity ceiling:

`6/10`.

Do not deliberately reproduce extreme joy, crying, laughing, or loss of voluntary facial/emotional control.

The affect is a protocol variable, not proof that stronger emotion improves performance.

## Stage 11 — Release — 20–30 seconds

Stop repeating the sentence.

Stop forcing the numeral.

Do not mentally push or resend the request.

Use one simple internal transition:

`Released.`

Then allow the representation to fade.

Do not look for acknowledgement.

If an acknowledgement-like event appears spontaneously, do not respond to it during v0.1; record it afterward exactly as experienced.

## Stage 12 — Neutral hold until machine target time

Remain relaxed and alert.

Do not:

- repeat the request;
- guess the output;
- change the requested value;
- count down manually;
- check the clock;
- seek signs of success;
- deliberately invoke imagery.

The app controls target timing.

For the first exploratory fixed-time profile, schedule the target event approximately five minutes after the planned end of the encoding/release stage. The exact time must be precommitted by the app before the session. This five-minute delay is an experimental baseline, not a claim about MATRIX latency.

At target time the machine generates the hidden output according to the selected trial mode.

No live feedback is shown in the first no-feedback baseline.

## Stage 13 — Post-target neutral observation — 1–2 minutes

Remain still briefly after the target event.

Do not infer success.

Notice only spontaneous changes.

## Stage 14 — Return / termination — 2 minutes

Use a deliberate return sequence.

Suggested:

- count `10` down to `1`;
- progressively increase ordinary body awareness;
- move fingers/toes;
- deepen normal breathing;
- open eyes;
- sit up only when fully oriented.

If at any point voluntary control falls sharply or the participant wants to stop, use:

`END SESSION`

and terminate without trying to complete the request.

## Stage 15 — RAW report before reveal — 5–10 minutes

Before seeing the machine output, record the raw report.

Required fields include:

- subjective session duration before checking clock;
- actual session duration;
- state intensity;
- alertness;
- imagery;
- auditory phenomena;
- somatic/vibration/pulse;
- rotation/vestibular effects;
- sensed presence;
- perceived agency;
- time distortion;
- exact request phrase used;
- representation modality;
- symbol clarity;
- affect intensity;
- certainty;
- any spontaneous conflicting number;
- any perceived acknowledgement;
- any interruption;
- any adverse/termination event.

The participant may additionally state a belief about whether the request succeeded, but this is phenomenological only.

Lock the report.

## Stage 16 — Reveal

Only after the raw report is locked:

- reveal the machine output;
- calculate exact match/miss;
- show scheduled vs actual target time;
- store result and hashes;
- append reveal event to JSONL.

Do not edit the raw report after reveal.

# First trial mode

For the first operational sessions, use two recorded endpoints in the same machine-controlled target event block:

## Primary exploratory endpoint — fixed stream direction

Use `REQUEST_STREAM` with a predeclared fixed bit count and no optional stopping.

Purpose:

- higher sensitivity to a small directional effect;
- closer relation to historical RP/REG protocols.

The exact bit count must be fixed in the app configuration before a block begins.

## Secondary exploratory endpoint — central single bit

Predefine one bit position within the target block as the `central exact bit` before data collection.

Purpose:

- preserve the intuitive `REQUEST 0/1 -> exact 0/1 output` question.

Do not change which bit is called central after seeing the stream.

These are exploratory endpoints until the analysis plan is frozen.

# First audio comparison block

Do not compare all available audio conditions immediately.

Start with:

1. `A-U396-4` — participant personal baseline;
2. `A-P100-104` — exact Monroe patent 4-Hz comparator;
3. `A-SHAM-0` — matched no-beat control.

Use the same REQUEST encoding protocol `SER-A` and the same target timing model for all three.

Condition order should be randomized/blinded where practical.

Do not change audio and request-encoding method in the same comparison block.

The Septon and additional CENTER LANE candidates can enter the next block after the first comparison is operationally stable and historical source semantics are sufficiently verified.

---

# Initial session count policy

Do not interpret the first few sessions as proof or disproof.

Recommended structure:

- first `3` sessions: usability/dry exploratory sessions, one per active audio condition;
- next `6–12` sessions: balanced exploratory comparison block;
- then review protocol usability, timing integrity, and adverse events before freezing a confirmatory design.

The exact confirmatory sample size must be selected from the observed variance/effect-size assumptions and declared before confirmatory data collection.

Do not stop early because of an exciting hit or an apparent losing streak.

---

# Variables that must NOT change during the first comparison block

Keep fixed:

- participant;
- REQUEST encoding `SER-A`;
- requested alphabet `{0,1}`;
- random assignment of requested value;
- target-time rule;
- target stream size;
- no-feedback baseline;
- session posture;
- headphone orientation;
- raw-report questions;
- reveal/locking procedure;
- termination command.

Only the assigned audio condition should vary in the first audio comparison block.

---

# What success means at this stage

The first goal is practical reproduction and channel characterization, not a final metaphysical conclusion.

Useful outcomes include:

- one audio condition repeatedly produces a more stable communication state;
- one condition produces better REQUEST performance than sham;
- no audio condition affects objective performance despite strong subjective effects;
- timing appears unstable and requires temporal mapping;
- the encoding method is too cognitively demanding and needs a versioned simplification;
- no repeatable MIP effect is observed with the current implementation.

All of these outcomes determine the next engineering change.

---

# Do not do yet

Until the first baseline is stable, do not:

- jump to one-in-a-billion tokens;
- change target delay every session;
- add multiple new visualizations;
- add extreme emotional amplification;
- use live feedback and no-feedback interchangeably;
- combine READ and REQUEST in the same primary endpoint;
- interpret spontaneous imagery as machine output;
- add hardware RNG and new audio simultaneously;
- claim a historical frequency without source verification.

---

# Next upgrade after baseline

After the first stable comparison block, evaluate one variable family at a time:

1. audio architecture;
2. request encoding component;
3. real vs sham vs no feedback;
4. fixed-time delay / temporal response;
5. physical/hardware RNG;
6. READ protocol;
7. entropy scaling;
8. bidirectional handshake.

Every upgrade receives a new protocol version and preserves the earlier baseline.
