## Status

## Purpose

This document defines a detailed participant procedure for an MIP REQUEST / INFLUENCE session that intentionally bridges two historically distinct lineages:

1. the 1979–1980 U.S. Army / SRI Remote Perturbation (RP) work, which supplied the machine-output REQUEST / INFLUENCE precedent; and
2. the Monroe / Gateway / CENTER LANE Hemi-Sync training lineage, which supplied altered-state preparation methods such as Energy Conversion Box, Resonant Tuning, REBAL, affirmation, Focus 10, and deliberate return.

This is **not** a claim that the Army used one universal second-by-second script identical to the one below. Public CENTER LANE records show that advanced Hemi-Sync training was individualized and specifically tailored beyond the public Gateway program. The exact complete custom script used with every CENTER LANE source has not been recovered.

The procedure below is therefore a **MIP operational reconstruction**. Each element is tagged by provenance so that historically documented material is not silently mixed with MIP-defined standardization.

# 2. Historical boundary: do not merge the two source families

## 2.1 Remote Perturbation was not a Gateway breathing experiment

`HIST-ARMY`

The 7 November 1979 Army Remote Perturbation protocol tested whether a participant could intentionally bias a random binary stream toward `0` or `1`. The participant could start successive trials at their own pace. The protocol explicitly excluded hypnosis, drugs, special auditory/visual/olfactory stimulation, subliminal stimulation, electrical stimulation, and electromagnetic stimulation.

Therefore, breathing, REBAL, Focus 10, and Hemi-Sync are **not** part of the historically documented 1979 RP induction procedure.

MIP deliberately combines the RP machine-output architecture with a separately sourced Gateway/CENTER LANE state-preparation procedure. That combination is experimental and must remain labeled as such.

## 2.2 CENTER LANE did use Monroe / Gateway / Hemi-Sync training

`HIST-ARMY`

Declassified CENTER LANE records document a four-phase Monroe-related training progression:

1. in-house use of the Monroe Discovery series;
2. attendance at the Gateway program;
3. one-on-one training with Robert Monroe under laboratory conditions, tailored to individual needs; and
4. use of specially designed Hemi-Sync environments during operational/applications training.

A 26 August 1983 CENTER LANE executive summary states that Phase III training was to be specifically tailored to the office and extend beyond the public MIAS program. It also describes biomonitoring, custom Hemi-Sync tape development, monitoring methods, controlled environments, and interpretation of information reported during Hemi-Sync work.

Therefore, the public Gateway sequence is a valid historical foundation, but it cannot be represented as the complete exact CENTER LANE custom procedure.

# 4. Environment and pre-session conditions

## 4.1 Environment

`HIST-ARMY + MIP-RECON`

CENTER LANE procedures specify ordinary ambient temperature and humidity, normal waking hours, and elimination of ordinary distractions such as radio, office machinery, and outside noise.

For MIP:

- use a quiet ordinary room;
- use normal comfortable temperature;
- perform during ordinary waking hours;
- silence calls and notifications;
- remove avoidable external distractions;
- do not use sleep deprivation as an induction method;
- do not use drugs or intoxicants;
- do not use forced hyperventilation;
- do not use unsafe sound levels.

## 4.2 Body position

`HIST-GATEWAY + MIP-RECON`

Use a relaxed position free of pressure points or muscular strain. Reclined or comfortably seated is acceptable if the same posture is used consistently within a comparison block.

Recommended MIP standard:

- head supported and approximately neutral;
- arms resting naturally;
- hands not gripping the device;
- legs uncrossed if comfortable;
- clothing loose enough not to create pressure distractions;
- eyes closed after the initial setup except for the documented Resonant Tuning eye-coordination step described below.

## 4.3 Headphones and audio

`HIST-GATEWAY + MIP-RECON`

Before START:

- verify left/right headphone orientation;
- verify both channels are audible;
- set a low comfortable volume;
- do not change the volume after commitment unless safety requires it;
- if audio becomes uncomfortable, distorted, painful, or startling, terminate the participant phase and record the event.

## 4.4 Target memory

`MIP-RECON`

Before START:

- read the assigned target carefully;
- memorize the exact participant-facing representation;
- confirm that the target can be recalled without looking at the screen;
- do not change the target after commitment;
- if the target is genuinely forgotten during the session, do not guess or substitute another value.

For a large integer such as `241627829`, the recommended first MIP representation is the exact digit string, optionally spoken internally digit-by-digit to avoid semantic rounding.

# 7. Energy Conversion Box

### `00:45–02:15`

`HIST-GATEWAY, standardized by MIP`

The public Gateway method uses a mentally constructed container in which distractions, worries, concerns, and physical preoccupations are set aside for the duration of the exercise.

## Exact MIP procedure

1. Imagine or sense a container in front of you.
2. It may be simple or elaborate; visual clarity is not required.
3. Open it.
4. Place into it, one at a time:
   - concern about whether the experiment will succeed;
   - fear of failure;
   - desire to force a result;
   - work/personal worries;
   - awareness of the clock;
   - awareness of the phone/computer;
   - unnecessary body-image concerns;
   - any expectation about what you "should" experience.
5. **Do not place the committed target in the box.** The target remains the single task item intentionally retained.
6. Close the container firmly.
7. Turn attention away from it.
8. Do not keep checking whether the contents are still there.

### Persian-use example

If the participant is worried about success, a concrete mental symbol can be used: imagine the word `نتیجه` written on a card, place the card in the box, close the lid, and turn away.

# 8. Resonant Tuning

### `02:15–05:15`

`HIST-GATEWAY, standardized by MIP`

The public Gateway guidance describes slightly deeper-than-normal breathing, imagined intake of fresh/vital energy, exhalation of stale/tired energy, and physical humming or singing on the exhale. Early Gateway instruction also coordinates eyes open on the inhale and eyes closed on the exhale.

For MIP v0.1, use **six comfortable cycles**. Do not hyperventilate.

## One standardized cycle

### Step 1 — Inhale

- Duration: approximately `4–5 seconds`.
- Inhale gently through the nose.
- Open the eyes during the inhale for this historical-orientation variant.
- Imagine an unlimited field of clean, bright, fresh energy around you.
- Sense it entering the entire body and moving upward toward the head.
- Do not force a specific color.

### Step 2 — Brief comfortable pause

- Duration: approximately `1 second`, maximum `2 seconds` if entirely comfortable.
- Do not strain or perform prolonged breath retention.
- Sense the inhaled energy settling or circulating gently in the head/body.

### Step 3 — Exhale with physical resonance

- Duration: approximately `6–8 seconds`.
- Close the eyes.
- Exhale through the mouth as though softly blowing out a candle.
- Use the **physical vocal cords** to hum or sustain a comfortable vowel/hum.
- The exact pitch is not important; do not force the throat.
- Imagine tired, stale, used-up tension leaving the body, especially downward and through the feet.

### Step 4 — Normal breathing

- Take one or two normal breaths before the next cycle.
- Remain relaxed.

Repeat the cycle six times at a comfortable pace.

## Stop rule

If light-headedness, tingling caused by overbreathing, air hunger, panic, or discomfort appears:

- stop Resonant Tuning immediately;
- return to normal breathing;
- do not "push through" to preserve the protocol;
- record the deviation after return.

# 9. Resonant Energy Balloon (REBAL)

### `05:15–07:15`

`HIST-GATEWAY, standardized by MIP`

Public Gateway guidance describes a circulating field in which energy flows outward from the top of the head, down and around the body, and returns through the feet, later taking on a spiral/circulating pattern.

## Exact MIP procedure

1. Keep the eyes closed.
2. Return to normal comfortable breathing.
3. On an inhale, sense the body as charged but calm.
4. On the exhale, imagine or feel a gentle stream rising through the top of the head like a fountain.
5. Let it flow down around the outside of the body on all sides.
6. Let it re-enter through the bottoms of the feet.
7. Let the circulation continue without muscular effort.
8. Repeat this complete circuit three times.
9. Then allow the field to become a continuous circulation or gentle spiral around the body.
10. Hold the representation lightly; do not strain to see it.

## Important historical boundary

No source used by MIP establishes a mandatory REBAL color. Therefore MIP v0.1 specifies **no required color**.

If a color appears spontaneously, observe it and report it after return; do not treat it as a required success marker.

# 11. Focus 10-style induction

### `08:15–14:15`

`HIST-GATEWAY structure + MIP timing`

Public Gateway training defines Focus 10 as approximately `mind awake / body asleep` and uses progressive relaxation while the participant remains mentally alert.

MIP does **not** claim that reaching this subjective state proves any special neurological state.

## Stage 11.1 — Head and face relaxation

### `08:15–09:15`

With eyes closed:

- relax forehead;
- relax eyelids and muscles around the eyes;
- unclench jaw;
- relax tongue;
- relax scalp;
- relax neck.

Do not deliberately slow breathing beyond comfort.

Internal instruction:

`Head and face: relax, let go.`

## Stage 11.2 — Internal quieting

### `09:15–09:45`

Sense the relaxation of the head becoming deeper and the internal verbal stream becoming quieter.

Mind remains alert. Do not try to suppress thoughts violently; let them pass.

## Stage 11.3 — Counted body sequence

The public Focus 10 training uses a numbered progressive relaxation sequence. MIP preserves the historical body-region order while standardizing the pace.

### Count 4 — Feet and toes — `09:45–10:15`

With closed eyes, direct attention to both feet and toes.

Internal instruction:

`Feet and toes: relax, let go, become still.`

### Count 5 — Legs — `10:15–10:45`

Move attention from hips through thighs, knees, calves, ankles, and feet.

`Both legs: relax, let go, become still.`

### Count 6 — Hips — `10:45–11:15`

Relax both hips and pelvic muscles without forcing posture.

### Count 7 — Lower body — `11:15–11:45`

Relax abdomen and lower trunk.

No special visualization of internal organs is required for MIP; simple whole-region relaxation is sufficient.

### Count 8 — Upper body — `11:45–12:15`

Relax chest, rib cage, diaphragm area, and upper trunk.

Breathing stays natural.

### Count 9 — Back — `12:15–12:45`

Move attention slowly along the back and spinal muscles.

Let supporting muscles release as far as posture safely permits.

### Count 10 — Arms, hands, fingers, shoulders — `12:45–13:15`

Relax shoulders, upper arms, forearms, hands, and fingers.

Then mentally repeat `10` three times slowly.

## Stage 11.4 — Hold the state

### `13:15–14:15`

Remain still.

Operational state description:

- body deeply relaxed;
- unnecessary movement reduced;
- mind awake and able to remember the target;
- no need to "prove" that the body is literally asleep.

If the participant falls asleep, record it after return rather than retroactively declaring the intended state achieved.

# 12. Participant-paced stabilization / connection period

### `14:15 onward — no fixed maximum inside this guide`

`MIP-RECON`

This is the key participant-paced section.

There is **no countdown to the request**.

The participant remains hands-free, eyes closed, and neutral until subjectively ready to perform the REQUEST.

## Readiness criteria

Proceed only when all are reasonably true:

- breathing is normal;
- body is comfortable and still;
- mind remains alert enough to remember the exact target;
- target can be recalled without strain;
- there is no need to rush because of the clock;
- there is no strong urge to force an extraordinary experience.

No particular imagery, vibration, rotation, light, color, pressure, presence, or "connection feeling" is required.

If such experiences arise, observe them without using them as proof that the channel is active.

## If readiness does not appear

The participant may simply remain neutral and eventually return.

A session with no REQUEST or an incomplete REQUEST is still valid procedural data and must not be deleted.

# 13. REQUEST micro-protocol

`MIP-RECON, based on REQUEST_ENCODING_V0.2`

The REQUEST starts **only when the participant chooses to begin it**.

The following internal micro-sequence is standardized to approximately 90 seconds. It is independent of the scientific target anchor unless a timing profile explicitly defines otherwise.

## `REQUEST +00 to +05 seconds` — Recall

Recall the exact target once.

Example target:

`241627829`

Do not look for a second target. Do not modify digits.

## `+05 to +15 seconds` — First semantic statement

Use a timing-neutral phrase compatible with owner-defined timing:

`My requested target is 241627829. Favor this value under the already committed timing policy.`

Persian operational equivalent:

`هدف من ۲۴۱۶۲۷۸۲۹ است. مطابق زمان‌بندی از پیش ثبت‌شده، این مقدار را ترجیح بده.`

## `+15 to +25 seconds` — Second repetition

Repeat the **same** statement internally.

Do not intensify the wording.

## `+25 to +35 seconds` — Third repetition

Repeat the same statement a third and final time.

After this point, stop verbal repetition.

## `+35 to +55 seconds` — Simple target representation

For a large integer:

- imagine the exact digit string written clearly on a neutral dark or plain background;
- keep it static rather than moving;
- if visual imagery is weak, internally pronounce the digits one by one;
- if both methods are used, keep them consistent and synchronized;
- do not create an elaborate story around the number.

Example:

`2 – 4 – 1 – 6 – 2 – 7 – 8 – 2 – 9`

The objective is stable representation, not vividness.

## `+55 to +70 seconds` — Completion/certainty tag

Allow a moderate sense that the REQUEST has been fully stated and does not need further effort.

Target intensity:

`4–6 / 10`

This is not excitement, euphoria, fear, or certainty that the experiment succeeded.

Useful internal meaning:

`The request is complete.`

## `+70 to +90 seconds` — Release

Stop actively trying to push the target.

Internal transition:

`Released.`

Then let the number, wording, and outcome expectation go.

Do not repeat the REQUEST again during this session unless a future versioned protocol explicitly requires repetition.

# 14. Neutral post-request observation

### `After REQUEST +90 seconds until participant return`

`HIST-GATEWAY principle + MIP-RECON`

Remain relaxed, eyes closed, and hands-free.

Do **not**:

- repeat the target;
- change the target;
- ask whether it worked;
- search for signs of success;
- inspect a clock;
- count seconds/minutes;
- touch the computer;
- deliberately generate dramatic imagery;
- treat spontaneous imagery as an objective machine result.

Simply observe.

If spontaneous imagery, sounds, body sensations, rotation, pressure, temperature changes, emotion, apparent acknowledgement, or other events occur, note them mentally and report them later.

The participant may remain here for as long as the committed operational session permits and personal comfort allows.

# 15. Deliberate return procedure

`HIST-GATEWAY return principle + MIP standardization`

The participant decides when to return unless a versioned protocol supplies an automatic return cue.

If the scientific timing profile uses participant Return/Stop as a reference, **the software Return/Stop event is the authoritative reference point**. The same return procedure should therefore be used consistently across comparable sessions.

## Return sequence

### Return `+00 to +20 seconds`

Begin an internal count from `10` down to `1`, approximately two seconds per number.

With each number, allow ordinary body awareness to increase.

### Around count 5

Begin moving fingers and toes gently.

### Around count 3

Take one or two slightly deeper, comfortable breaths.

### At count 1

- open the eyes;
- look around the room;
- identify where you are;
- notice normal physical orientation.

### Next `20–40 seconds`

- sit up slowly if reclined;
- continue moving hands/feet;
- breathe normally;
- confirm that vision and balance feel ordinary;
- do not rush to stand.

### Software Return/Stop

When sufficiently oriented to interact safely with the device, press the formal Return/Stop action **once**.

Do not close the application afterward. The committed evidence phase may intentionally continue after participant return.

# 18. Abort / termination procedure

`HIST-ARMY safety principle + MIP safety doctrine`

At any time, safety takes priority over protocol completion.

If the participant experiences significant discomfort, panic, severe dizziness, pain, unsafe audio, disorientation, or simply chooses to stop:

1. open the eyes;
2. remove/lift headphones if desired;
3. return to ordinary breathing;
4. move fingers and toes;
5. orient visually to the room;
6. sit up slowly;
7. when safe, use the application Return/Abort action;
8. record the reason and approximate phase;
9. preserve the session data.

Do not delete an aborted session.

- repeat the REQUEST after release;

- inspect the system outcome before report lock;
