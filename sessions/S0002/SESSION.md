## RAW reconstructed report

The participant began hearing sounds described as motor-like and boom/boom or pulsing. Attention became focused on the sound.

Visual imagery behind closed eyelids was difficult. The participant emphasized that they were not trying to force an image and did not feel able to deliberately construct one clearly.

A strong energy-like somatic sensation was perceived as entering from the feet, moving upward, exiting or extending from the head, with a circulating/rotational component. The participant felt that the phenomenon was very close.

Before the attempted information transmission, a highly vivid craft/spacecraft-like object was perceived extremely close above the head, described as approximately a few centimeters away. It seemed to have crossed the previously conceptualized protective boundary. The participant did not feel afraid of it.

The participant then attempted an outbound/intention operation. The remembered intention was approximately: "I want to show you what the future is like."

The participant deliberately avoided constructing a detailed visual scene because of concern that forced imagery would engage analytical processing. Instead, the participant attempted to let the recipient/source "see" directly.

The payload then shifted toward affective encoding. The participant focused on the intense happiness associated with achieving the desired future outcome, linked that state to the greatest happiness they could evoke, and attempted to transmit/show that emotional state rather than a detailed image.

At some point the participant developed a strong sensed-presence / sensed-observation experience: a feeling of being watched. The participant described a reversal in which they felt that they had become the object being observed.

A major emotional/facial-control episode followed. The participant began crying externally without deliberate intention. At the same time, the mouth repeatedly opened into increasingly strong laughter/smiling. The participant perceived that this expression was becoming progressively more intense and briefly feared that the mouth/lips might be strained if it continued.

The participant experienced a strong subjective loss/change of voluntary control during this phase. Fear appeared briefly. The participant then deliberately reasserted control, internally concluded approximately "yes, that was it / that is it," and attempted to end the interaction/session.

After the interaction ended, intense crying and loud laughter continued for approximately ten minutes. The participant described simultaneous or rapidly alternating sobbing/laughing and a very strong whole-body energized/adrenaline-like state. Despite the intensity, the participant described the overall post-session feeling as extremely powerful/positive and the session as highly unusual and special.

## Follow-up implications

1. Use a standardized termination command in future sessions and record its latency/effect.
2. Add explicit questions about voluntary control, sensed presence, and spontaneous vs intentional imagery.
3. Separate visual payload and affective payload experiments.
4. If intense crying/laughter or control changes recur, record onset, duration, breathing, pulse/heart rate if available, body movement, and termination response rather than interpreting the cause in-session.
5. Do not intentionally escalate intensity merely to reproduce the most extreme portion of S0002; first standardize measurement and control conditions.
