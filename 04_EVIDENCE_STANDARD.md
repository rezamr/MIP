# MIP Evidence Standard

## Purpose

This document defines the evidentiary rules used across all MIP research and experiments.

## Evidence ladder

### L0 — Single subjective observation
A single reported experience without replication or objective validation.

### L1 — Repeated subjective observation
A similar phenomenon recurs across multiple sessions.

### L2 — Association with experimental condition
The phenomenon occurs preferentially under a defined condition relative to control/sham.

### L3 — Blinded above-chance result
A predeclared outcome exceeds the null expectation under blinded conditions.

### L4 — Preregistered replication
The result repeats under a preregistered protocol and frozen analysis plan.

### L5 — Independent replication
An independent team or independently administered dataset reproduces the effect.

### L6 — Mechanism-sensitive replication
The effect changes predictably when a hypothesized mechanism is manipulated while competing explanations are controlled.

## Claim classes

Use one of the following where applicable:

- `OBSERVATION`
- `PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT`
- `SUPPORTED SCIENCE`
- `MIXED / INCONCLUSIVE`
- `HISTORICAL CLAIM`
- `RECONSTRUCTED`
- `EXPERIMENTAL`
- `SPECULATIVE`
- `UNKNOWN`
- `UNRESOLVED`
- `CONTRADICTED`
- `FAILED HYPOTHESIS`

## Source hierarchy

### Tier 1
- original government documents;
- original patents;
- original archival records;
- original writings;
- contemporaneous correspondence;
- peer-reviewed primary research.

### Tier 2
- systematic reviews;
- meta-analyses;
- academic books;
- university archives;
- professional historical scholarship.

### Tier 3
- high-quality investigative journalism;
- institutional histories.

### Tier 4
- biographies;
- reputable secondary reporting;
- popular-science summaries.

### Tier 5
- blogs;
- commercial wellness sites;
- YouTube;
- Reddit;
- social media;
- unsourced websites.

Tier 5 may be useful for tracing the origin or spread of a claim, but not as scientific proof.

## Critical distinction rules

Do not convert:

- `the document says X` into `X is true`;
- `a patent claims X` into `X was implemented`;
- `a government program investigated X` into `X worked`;
- `a participant experienced X` into `X had an external cause`;
- `two systems share a frequency` into `the systems are causally linked`;
- `a source is missing` into `the source was deliberately suppressed`.

## Session evidence rules

Every session result must preserve:

- what was planned;
- what actually happened;
- raw observations;
- interruptions;
- nulls and misses;
- late recollections;
- target/reveal timing when applicable;
- condition/blinding status;
- exact audio settings when known;
- confidence ratings;
- analysis performed later.

## Information-transfer evidence rules

A strong anomalous-information claim should progressively survive:

1. target secrecy;
2. participant blinding where possible;
3. experimenter blinding where possible;
4. machine-generated target selection;
5. raw-response lock before reveal;
6. independent or algorithmic scoring;
7. matched decoys or forced-choice designs;
8. prespecified primary endpoint;
9. multiple-comparison control;
10. replication with a new target set;
11. independent replication;
12. sensory-leakage audit.

## Negative-evidence rule

Negative findings are mandatory project data.

Examples:

- no phenomenon under active audio;
- phenomenon under sham audio;
- target miss;
- historically claimed parameter not found;
- proposed cross-program link unsupported;
- previously strong hypothesis weakened by new evidence.

Never hide negative findings to protect a preferred theory.

## Unknown-parameter rule

If an exact historical parameter cannot be established, write `UNKNOWN` and preserve:

- what is known;
- candidate range;
- source clues;
- why the value matters;
- how it might be recovered;
- whether an experimental substitute is being used.

An experimental substitute must never be described as a recovered historical value.

## Retrospective-memory rule

A session reconstructed from memory must be labeled `RECONSTRUCTED FROM MEMORY` and should never be treated as equivalent to a contemporaneously locked raw record.

## Interpretation discipline

Every unusual event should support multiple candidate interpretations until evidence discriminates among them.

The project may investigate unconventional hypotheses, but evidentiary thresholds do not change because a hypothesis is interesting.
